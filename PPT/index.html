The problem

A neighbour needs help. A group message may miss the person closest to them.

Who can help nearby?

A message rarely tells us who has the right skills or whether someone has already responded.

What is happening now?

The location and latest update can get lost among forwards and replies.

Our project focuses on the gap between asking for help and finding a willing local responder.

NeighbourAid3 / 10

The idea

One report with a location, a clear urgency label and a visible response status.

1

Report

Describe the incident and confirm the map location.

2

Find help

Nearby volunteers receive alerts based on distance and relevant skills.

3

Respond

A volunteer accepts and shares an estimated arrival time.

4

Update

The reporter follows progress until the responder marks the alert resolved.

The app supports community coordination. It does not replace emergency services.

NeighbourAid4 / 10

Emergency help

The details a responder needs, kept together.

  • Map and location: nearby alerts, full-screen view and filters below the map.
  • Response details: acceptance, updates and optional contact after acceptance.
  • Weak connectivity: save a report locally and retry while the app is open.
  • Medical support: nearby hospital listings and a user-confirmed emergency call.

Queued does not mean delivered. Hospital listings do not confirm bed or ambulance availability.

Everyday help

A separate help board for paid work, away from the emergency feed.

Home services

Plumbing, electrical work, repairs and other local jobs.

Computer support

Device setup, computer repair and help with technical problems.

A simple request

Post the task and budget, review offers, choose a worker and follow the work timeline.

Visit times are preferences. The app does not process payments or guarantee a worker's availability.

NeighbourAid6 / 10

The role of AI

AI helps organise a report. A person still decides how to respond.

  • First pass: rules and multilingual phrases assign an urgency level.
  • Optional local model: Gemma can raise urgency when the first pass misses implied danger.
  • Clearer headlines: guardrails check numbers and negation before accepting a rewrite.
  • Photo checks: uncertain or mixed evidence stays inconclusive.

Models run on the backend when configured, not on the phone. Scores are signals, not proof that a report is true.

The separate demo uses fictional browser data and does not connect to the real database.

A small pilot

We propose testing with one campus or neighbourhood before wider use.

  • Recruit willing volunteers and record the skills they can safely offer.
  • Use clearly labelled drills to practise reporting and acceptance.
  • Ask users about confusing steps, missed alerts and location errors.
  • Review privacy and notification reliability before inviting more people.

This is a pilot proposal. We are not claiming live adoption, emergency response times or field accuracy.
